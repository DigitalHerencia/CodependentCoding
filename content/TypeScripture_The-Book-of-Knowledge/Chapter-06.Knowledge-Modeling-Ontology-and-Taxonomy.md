# Chapter 06: Knowledge Modeling — Ontology and Taxonomy

**The Book of Knowledge™**

## Knowledge-model stack

- Ontology: what exists and relates.
- Taxonomy: how known things are classified.
- Terminology/nomenclature: what concepts are called.
- Semantics: what names and contracts mean.
- Mereology: part-whole relationships.
- Topology: connection and dependency structure.
- Epistemology: what counts as evidence and authority.
- Typology: recurring kinds/patterns.
- Information architecture: how humans and machines recover the knowledge.

## Rule

- Do not force every conceptual dimension into the filesystem. Classification belongs in the model and metadata unless physical separation materially improves retrieval or enforcement.
- A folder is not an ontology, and an ontology is not a reason to create six levels of folders.

## Taxonomy principle

- Classification criteria must be explicit enough that two readers place the same artifact the same way. If classification does not affect retrieval, ownership, authority, or execution, it may not deserve a separate physical category.
