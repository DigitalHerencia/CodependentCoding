# Chapter 01: Engineering Practice — Descriptive Model

**The Book of Implementation™**

## Canonical procedure

1. Define the subject, period, system class, and exclusions.
2. Inventory direct statements, formal rules, implementations, tests, incidents, and operational evidence.
3. Trace representative runtime paths from entrypoint to state change or rendered result.
4. Map roles, responsibilities, boundaries, contracts, invariants, and truth owners.
5. Separate recurring principles from product-specific or historical accidents.
6. Record contradictions instead of silently reconciling them.
7. Produce the descriptive model first; derive governance only after the model is understood.

## Working record

- For each consequential claim record: `claim`, `mode`, `source`, `observed evidence`, `confidence`, `contradictions`, and `disposition`.
- The record is analysis support, not another authority layer.

## Completion test

- A technically capable reader should be able to predict likely decisions in unfamiliar cases and explain why those decisions follow from the model.
